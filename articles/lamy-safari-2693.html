On Lamy Safari, and What It Says About Us

Everyone has an object they reach for without thinking. For 14 months, that object has been Lamy Safari. I did not plan for this. It simply happened, one morning at a time, until the object became as automatic as breathing.

It arrived on a Monday morning. The box was heavier than expected. That is usually a good sign, though not always. I have learned to distrust light packages. They promise lightness and deliver disappointment. Lamy Safari did not promise lightness. It promised mass, and it delivered.

The price of Lamy Safari annoyed me at first. It seemed expensive. Then I calculated how many cheaper alternatives I would have to buy over the same period, and the math changed. Cheap is expensive when you have to buy it again.

I do not remember the exact day I decided I needed Lamy Safari. Decisions like that accrete, like sediment, until one day you realize you have already made them and are merely waiting for the delivery confirmation.
