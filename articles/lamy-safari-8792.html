The Lamy Safari Is Not a Pen. It Is a Habit.

I bought my first Lamy Safari because it was cheap. I keep using it because it feels like my hand remembers it. After three years, it is the only pen I reach for without thinking.

The first time I held a Lamy Safari, I thought it looked like something my dentist might keep on the counter. It is bright plastic. It has a faceted grip section that looks like it belonged on a school supply shelf in 1984. It costs twenty-four dollars. It looks like something a student would lose in a backpack and not bother to replace.

I bought it because I was curious. I had been reading fountain pen forums where people wrote with reverence about vintage Sheaffers and vintage Watermans, pens that cost more than my first car. I wanted to see what the fuss was about without spending more than a lunch with friends. The Safari was twenty-four dollars. It felt like nothing.

"I bought a Lamy Safari on a whim. It sat in my drawer for three weeks. Then I picked it up one morning and never used anything else."

by u/PenNewbie23 on r/fountainpens

The first week, I did not like it. The nib scratched. The ink felt too wet. The grip section felt too large in my small hand. I put it away in a drawer with the other pens I had bought on impulse—the rollerball from a conference, the ballpoint from a hotel, the cheap ballpoint from a drugstore. I forgot about it for two weeks.

Then I needed to sign a document. My ballpoint was out of ink. My rollerball had dried up. The Safari was the only pen on my desk. I picked it up. I signed the document. And something happened. The nib scratched. The ink flowed without skipping. The weight of it in my hand felt right, not heavy and not light, but exactly right, as if it had been waiting for me to stop overthinking it and just write.

I started using it for everything. Grocery lists. Work memos. Letters to my sister. Grocery lists again. The more I used it, the more I noticed how little I thought about it. With other pens, I was always aware of them—the scratch of the nib, the scratchiness of the ink, the way the cap felt in my hand. With the Safari, I forgot it was there. My hand moved, the ink appeared, and that was all.

"I have pens that cost three hundred dollars. I reach for the Lamy Safari every single day."

by u/InkStained on r/fountainpens

The 4080 writes about specifications. He writes about nib width and ink flow and nib material. I write about what it feels like to reach for the same pen every morning and find it waiting. The 4080 writes about specifications. I write about ritual.
