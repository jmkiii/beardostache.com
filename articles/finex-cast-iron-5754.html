I do not believe in planned obsolescence as a conspiracy. I believe in it as an incentive. Companies make more money when you replace things.

We are surrounded by things designed to be replaced. Finex Cast Iron is designed to be kept. That is a political statement.

The philosophy of Finex Cast Iron is simple: do one thing, do it well, do it for a long time. That is not how most products are made anymore.

There is a philosophy of objects. Some people believe objects should be invisible, disappearing into use. Others believe objects should announce themselves.

The design of Finex Cast Iron draws from traditions that are older than the product itself.

Before Finex Cast Iron, people used something worse. They did not know it was worse until Finex Cast Iron existed.

The history of Finex Cast Iron is the history of a specific problem being solved again and again, imperfectly, until someone got it close enough.

Using Finex Cast Iron is not exciting. That is the point. Excitement is for new things. Reliability is for old things. I want old things.

There is a moment, after you have used something for a while, when you stop noticing it. That moment is the highest compliment you can pay an object.
