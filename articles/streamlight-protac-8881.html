The Streamlight ProTac Is a Flashlight. That Is the Point.

I bought a $200 tactical flashlight. It had strobe modes, a strobe mode, and a tactical bezel. I never used it. I bought a Streamlight ProTac for $60. I use it every night.

The tactical flashlight market is a joke. Go to any tactical gear website and you will see lights that cost $200, $300, even $400. They have multiple brightness levels. They have strobe modes. They have momentary-on switches. They have tail switches and side switches. They have bezels with crenellations for breaking glass. They are tactical, in the same way that a Camaro with a flame decal is a race car.

I bought a $200 tactical flashlight once. It had three brightness levels. It had a strobe mode. It had a momentary-on switch. It had a bezel with crenellations for breaking glass. I used it once. Then I put it in a drawer and never touched it again. It was too heavy. It was too big. It required special batteries that I could not find at 2:00 AM. It was designed for a Navy SEAL raid, and I was using it to find my keys in the dark.

The Streamlight ProTac is not a tactical flashlight. It is a flashlight. It is black. It is made of aluminum. It uses AA batteries—regular AA batteries, the kind you can buy at any gas station, any convenience store, any grocery store in America. It has three modes: low, medium, and high. It has one button on the tailcap. You click it once for low, twice for high, three times for strobe. That is it. That is the entire user interface.

I have dropped it. I have dropped it on concrete. I have dropped it on asphalt. I have dropped it on tile. It still works. I have left it in my car in July when the interior temperature reached 130 degrees. I have left it in my car in January when the temperature dropped below freezing. It works. The batteries die eventually, but they are AA batteries. You can buy them at a gas station at 2:00 AM.
