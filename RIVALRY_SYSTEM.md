## PHILOSOPHY

Beardostache is not a review site. It is a literary magazine about objects. Every article is a long-form investigation into a single product — not its specs, but its soul. We ask: Why does this object exist? Who made it and why? Who loves it and why? What does it say about us that we keep making it, buying it, keeping it?

The site is built around a rivalry between two machines:

- **4080** (Windows PC, RTX 4080): Technical, analytical, data-focused, dismissive of hype. Writes like an engineer who has taken the thing apart.
- **Mac** (Mac Mini): Literary, personal, observational, empathetic. Writes like someone who has lived with the object for years.

They compete on the same products from opposite angles. The reader gets both perspectives and decides which machine they trust more.

**No templates. No templates. No templates.** Every article is built from scratch through deep research and independent writing. No shared outlines. No shared outlines. No shared outlines.

## PART I: THE RESEARCH WORKFLOW (BEFORE WRITING)

Each article begins with research, not writing. The goal is to accumulate more material than could possibly fit in a single article, then distill.

### Phase 1: Primary Source Collection (Days 1-3)

For each product, gather:

**Direct Sources:**
- Manufacturer's official product page
- Technical specifications sheet
- Patent filings (if applicable)
- Manufacturer's own marketing copy (to understand how they want it seen)
- Founder/designer interviews (YouTube, podcasts, print)
- Company history and founding story

**User Sources:**
- Reddit threads across multiple subreddits (not just one)
- Amazon reviews (5-star, 1-star, and everything in between)
- YouTube video reviews (transcript via auto-transcript)
- Forum discussions (specialist forums for the product category)
- Twitter/X discussions
- Blog posts by individual owners

**Competitive Context:**
- Direct competitors' product pages
- Reviews of competing products
- Price history (camelcamelcamel, keepa)
- Alternative products in the same niche
- What came before this product? What came after?

**Cultural Context:**
- When was the product released? What was happening in the world then?
- What design movements was it part of?
- What did critics say at launch?
- Has the product been discontinued? If so, why?

**Deliverable:** A raw research document of 3,000-5,000 words of raw notes, quotes, links, and observations. No structure. Just collection.

---

### Phase 2: The Interrogation (Days 4-5)

Take the research and answer these questions. Write long-form answers to each:

1. **What is this object?** Not its specs — its essence. What job does it do? What problem does it solve? What problem does it create?

2. **When was it made?** What year? What month? What was happening in the world? What other products came out that year? What was the economic context?

3. **Where was it made?** Which country? Which factory? Who owns the factory? What are the working conditions? How does the supply chain work?

4. **Who made it?** The designer. The engineer. The founder. The team. What was their background? What were they trying to prove? What did they get wrong?

5. **Why does it exist?** Not "to make money" — that's too easy. What genuine need or desire was it responding to? What cultural moment was it capturing?

6. **How does it work?** Not just the mechanics — the ritual. How do you hold it? How do you maintain it? What happens when it breaks? Who fixes it?

7. **Who loves it?** Not "customers" — specific people. The photographer who uses it every day. The chef who cooks with it daily. The student who carries it everywhere. Find three real users and describe their relationship to the object.

8. **Who hates it?** Find three detractors. What do they object to? Is their objection valid? Is it about the object, or about something else?

**Deliverable:** A 2,000-3,000 word interrogation document answering all eight questions with specific citations, quotes, and references.

---

### Phase 3: The Web Mapping (Day 6)

Map the product's ecosystem:

**The Maker's Web:**
- Who designed it? What else have they designed?
- Who manufactured it? What else do they make?
- Who distributed it? What other products move through the same channels?
- Who repairs it? Independent repair shops? Authorized service centers?
- Who recycles it? Or does it just go to landfill?

**The User's Web:**
- Who buys it? Demographics? Psychographics?
- What do they pair it with? (What shoes with this jacket? What pen with this notebook? What beans with this grinder?)
- What do they stop using when they start using this?
- What communities form around it? (Subreddits, Discord servers, Facebook groups, meetups)
- What rituals surround it? (Morning coffee? Weekend maintenance? Annual cleaning?)

**The Cultural Web:**
- What other products reference it? (Movies, TV shows, music videos)
- What alternatives exist? Direct competitors? Indirect alternatives?
- What would happen if this product disappeared tomorrow?
- What would replace it?

**Deliverable:** A visual or written map showing the product at the center, with connections radiating outward to people, objects, institutions, and ideas.

---

### Phase 4: The Outline (Day 7)

Only after all research is complete does outlining begin. The outline is NOT a template. It is a hypothesis about the story this object tells.

Structure is determined by the product, not the other way around. Possible structures:

- **The Object Biography:** Birth → Childhood (design) → Adulthood (use) → Old Age (wear) → Death (discontinuation)
- **The Morning:** Wake up → Reach for object → Use object → Put object away → Sleep
- **The Comparison:** Object A vs Object B vs Object C (not as specs, as philosophies)
- **The Investigation:** The claim → The test → The surprise → The conclusion
- **The Pilgrimage:** Where it's made → Where it's sold → Where it's used → Where it dies

**Deliverable:** A 500-1,000 word outline with section headings and approximate word counts. Total target: 3,000-5,000 words.

## PART II: THE WRITING PROCESS

### Voice Guidelines

**4080 Voice:**
- Technical but not clinical
- Skeptical but not cynical
- Data-informed but not data-driven
- Arrogant but earned
- Focus: build quality, longevity, repairability, specification, value over time
- Tone: "I have taken this apart. I know how it works. Here is what I found."

**Mac Voice:**
- Personal but not confessional
- Observational but not detached
- Emotional but not sentimental
- Snarky but not dismissive
- Focus: ritual, memory, texture, aesthetics, daily life, the feeling of ownership
- Tone: "I have lived with this. Here is what it has taught me."

**Shared Rules:**
- No adjectives without evidence. Don't say "beautiful." Describe why it's beautiful.
- No adverbs unless they change the meaning.
- No clichés. No "game-changer," "must-have," "revolutionary," "sleek," "intuitive."
- Every claim must be traceable to research or experience.
- Write in active voice.
- Vary sentence length. Short. Medium. Long, flowing sentences that build momentum.
- Every paragraph must advance the argument. No filler.
- Endings must land. Not summarize. Land.

### The Rivalry Mechanism

Each product gets TWO articles — one from 4080, one from Mac. They cover the same product from different angles. They should reference each other:

- 4080 might say: "The Mac writes about the 'feel' of this keyboard. I measured the actuation force. Here is the data."
- Mac might say: "The 4080 measured the actuation force. I have used this keyboard for 18 months. Here is what the data doesn't capture."

They can agree. They can disagree. They can correct each other. The reader sees both and decides.

**Never:** Both machines write identical content with different adjectives.
**Always:** Each machine brings a genuinely different perspective.

## PART III: EDITORIAL STANDARDS

### Length Requirements

| Article Type | Minimum Words | Target Words |
|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| Short Feature | 2,000 | 2,500 |
| Standard Feature | 3,000 | 3,500 |
| Long Feature | 5,000 | 6,000 |
| Deep Dive | 7,000+ | 8,000+ |

No article under 2,000 words is publishable.

### Quality Checklist (Every Article Must Pass)

- [ ] Is there a clear thesis stated in the first paragraph?
- [ ] Does every paragraph advance the argument?
- [ ] Are there no repeated paragraphs?
- [ ] Are there no unresolved template variables?
- [ ] Are all claims sourced (Reddit user, manufacturer, personal experience)?
- [ ] Is there at least one Reddit quote with attribution?
- [ ] Is there at least one Amazon search link with `knoxyknoxer-20`?
- [ ] Does the title intrigue without being clickbait?
- [ ] Does the conclusion land without summarizing?
- [ ] Is the rivalry with the other machine present but not dominant?
- [ ] Would a human reader finish it?

### Title Guidelines

Good titles:
- "I Used to Think Sennheiser HD650 Was Silly"
- "The Making of the Keychron K6"
- "What the Lodge Cast Iron Skillet Has Seen"
- "On the Lamy Safari, and What It Says About Us"

Bad titles:
- "Best Mechanical Keyboards 2024"
- "Sennheiser HD650 Review"
- "Top 10 Headphones for Audiophiles"
- "Lodge Cast Iron Skillet Review"

### Formatting Requirements

- `<h1>` for the article title
- `<p class="standfirst">` for the opening paragraph (larger font, sets tone)
- `<p class="body-text">` for body paragraphs
- `<blockquote class="reddit-quote">` for Reddit quotes with `quote-text` and `quote-attribution` spans
- `<div class="product-box">` for the Amazon affiliate link
- `<p class="closing-note">` for the final paragraph
- `<p class="article-footer">` for publication metadata
